# Writing standards

A supplemental report is a factual record that a league or disciplinary committee may rely on in a hearing. It states what happened. It does not argue, defend, or conclude.

## Rules

- **First person, past tense, active voice, in order.** "I stopped play and cautioned #7 for unsporting behavior."
- **Actions, not labels.** Write what was done: "tackled from behind with studs raised after the ball had been played away." The misconduct reason can follow in parentheses: "(reckless foul)", "(dissent)".
- **Words verbatim.** Quote exactly, profanity included. Don't paraphrase "f***ing terrible" as "used foul language."
- **Attribute every observation.** "I saw." "Assistant referee Sam Chen reported that." "My view was blocked by two players." Never present something you were told as something you saw.
- **Sequence explains restarts.** If an offense came before a foul and before the whistle, say so in order; the restart follows the first offense. Let the order of events answer "why no penalty kick?", not commentary.
- **Name people clearly.** Full name and team at first mention (in the subject block), then a short form ("#7", "Coach Smith"). With two coaches involved, never just "the coach."
- **Times as logged.** Use the minutes from the timing app. If they could look odd (a 65th minute in a 60-minute match), add a one-line Match Timing note explaining the clock. Don't shift minutes after the fact.
- **Cite carefully.** Don't cite a Law number or league rule you haven't verified. "Under the current Laws of the Game" is safer than a guessed number.

## Leave out

- Opinions about character or attitude: aggressive, belligerent, irate, disrespectful, unacceptable, unsportsmanlike (as a description, not a code)
- Mind-reading: deliberately, intentionally, wanted to, tried to provoke, clearly, obviously
- Feelings: "I felt", "I was concerned"
- Hypotheticals: "would have been a penalty if..."
- Self-defense: "I had no obligation to", "I was well positioned"
- Admissions that change nothing: "I didn't see the flag at first"
- Analysis frameworks (the 3Ps of dissent, DOGSO considerations) as text. Use them to choose the decision, and let the facts show it.
- Labels with a formal meaning you aren't asserting as a separate allegation, such as "abuse" or "assault". Those start a different process. Describe the conduct; file the separate report if it applies.
- Requests for sanctions. The facts are enough.

## Specific situations

- **Rescinded or changed decisions.** State the original decision, who asked or what prompted the change, the fact that drove it, and that it happened before play resumed or the match was abandoned. Don't claim discretion the Laws don't give. Example: "I was not obligated to grant the request. Considering the welfare of the players, I decided to rescind the second caution and the dismissal before play resumed or the match was abandoned." Use the same wording in the Misconduct line.
- **Team official enters the field.** Non-confrontational entry is usually warning-level. If a caution followed, it's for what came next (for example, refusing an instruction). Write the sequence that way.
- **Second caution.** Describe both cautions in full.
- **AR-sourced cautions.** Name the AR, say the referee's view was blocked if it was, and summarize what the AR reported. Ask the AR to file their own supplemental report.

## Tone check before delivery

Read the draft and fix any of these:

1. Any adjective or adverb about a person rather than an action
2. Any claim about intent or state of mind
3. Any sentence the referee couldn't say "I saw or heard that" about, unless it's attributed
4. Any sentence that argues for the decision instead of describing what happened
5. Any word that was paraphrased when the exact words are known

Then ask the referee: "Is there anything in here you didn't see or hear yourself, or that you'd have trouble repeating in a hearing?"

## Before and after

Before:
> The coach was completely out of line and aggressively yelled at me about a call I clearly got right. I had no choice but to card her, which she deserved.

After:
> Coach Smith shouted, "Call it both ways, ref!" after a challenge for the ball on the previous play, for which I had not awarded a foul. At the next stoppage I went to the technical area and said, "Coach, that's enough." She replied, "I'll say whatever I want." I cautioned her for dissent.
